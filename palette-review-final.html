Stimulus palette review

Candidate color sets for the numerosity task, with live dot arrays, perceptual separation metrics, and color-vision simulation. The interface is deliberately achromatic so that nothing induces a contrast shift in the swatches.

Distances are Euclidean in CAM16-UCS. Contrast is Michelson luminance contrast against the background. Viewing conditions: D65, LA 4.07 cd/m², Yb 20, average surround.

Each field holds the same number of dots of every color, and either field can be swapped to any candidate set. If a set is well balanced, no color should read as the most numerous.

Background Luminance

A background at the same lightness as the dots leaves them with no luminance contrast at all, so their edges exist only in the chromatic channel. Chromatic spatial resolution is several times coarser than luminance resolution, which is why isoluminant dots look soft and unstable and are hard to individuate — the same property that makes isoluminance a tool for silencing the magnocellular pathway.

Offsetting the background fixes it. The colors stay equiluminant to one another, which is what keeps salience equal, but every dot now carries the same large luminance contrast against the surround. Both panels below hold the identical Equiluminant 4 set in the identical arrangement; only the surround differs.

Each set's recommended background puts its mean luminance at a Michelson contrast of 0.76, roughly 38 L* below the set itself. Darker would raise the number further — at pure black every contrast tends to 1.00 no matter how dim the dot — but that is an artifact of the measure rather than a real improvement, and it puts the surround on the panel's black floor where the displayed value is least trustworthy. For a set whose colors already share a luminance, any neutral surround equalizes contrast automatically; the recommendation only decides how much.

All candidates, side by side

Minimum separation sets the hardest discrimination in the set. Uniformity is the ratio of the largest pairwise distance to the smallest — 1.00 would mean every pair is equally far apart. Contrast is the range across colors of Michelson luminance contrast against the current background: a wide range means some colors are simply easier to see than others, independent of hue. Thus a small range is ideal.
